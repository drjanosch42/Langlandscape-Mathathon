## Recurring patterns
- The failures were in coordination, not in the mathematics: dialogs, delayed messages, stale bases, wrong directories.
  Each became a check or a rule in `agents/` within minutes, so the process got more robust during the day.
- Every channel that writes to git was reliable; the in-session channel was not.
- Remote-only builds make the base commit of each round matter: most wasted rounds came from stale bases, not from the WP itself.
- Stages 3–6: wall time was set by the number of remote rounds, not by writing or proof difficulty; two build nodes with single-WP lettered jobs gave 2–4 min feedback (S03: 22 rounds in 52 min; S04: 49 rows in 112 min).
- Rules that paid off, in order of adoption: file outbox (S01), early gate, pregate on the merged tree (S05, S06), Fable audits in parts with in-stage fix WPs (S04–S06), rescue writers at round 2–3 (S03–S04), second readers on the other account (S06).
- Honesty rules became process: every hypothesis whose statement implies its use's conclusion is disclosed as such (S06), every relaxed HACS step is listed as a deviation (see [hacs.md](hacs.md)), and nothing computed is called certified.
