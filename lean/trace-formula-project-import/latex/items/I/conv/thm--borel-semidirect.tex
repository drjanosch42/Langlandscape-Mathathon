Let $R$ be a commutative ring. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item every $b\in B(R)$ can be written as $b=tn$ with $t\in T(R)$ and $n\in N(R)$, and these $t$, $n$ are unique;
  explicitly, $\begin{psmallmatrix}a&y\\0&d\end{psmallmatrix}=\diag(a,d)\,n(a^{-1}y)$, where $a,d\in R^\times$;
\item $T(R)\cap N(R)=\{1\}$;
\item $N(R)$ is a normal subgroup of $B(R)$.
\end{enumerate}
