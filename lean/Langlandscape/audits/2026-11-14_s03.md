`|X_B(Ô^×)| = 1`). This is `lem:s03-elliptic` in the tex's own "that is" form, plus the `q ∈ {2,3}` clause of
`prop:s03-mazur`. `DefiniteData` carries `RamifiedExactly a b D` and a maximal order (`Quat/Basic.lean:54–64`), so the
statement is about the right algebra and is true for every `d` with `D` prime; it is vacuous only for composite `D`.

`[x_c y] = c'` (`AlgJL/VolIII/Brandt/Brandt.lean:621–625`), i.e. the neighbours of `c` in the class `c'`, so this is
`w_e ∣ B(e,e')` of `lem:s03-stabfree` at `M = 1`, with the right index order. True by the tex proof, hence non-vacuous.

texts are wrong: see **R1**.

### 2.4 `hsym` in the level-raising identity

`Graph.alpha_alphaDagger_eq` (`Graph/LevelRaising.lean:137`) assumes `s₀* s₁† = s₁* s₀†` on basis vectors. The
assumption is necessary: the upper right entry of `α α†` is `s₀* s₁† = T_q†`, and the tex states `T_q† = T_q` only "at
level `U₀(M)`".

`Ribet.levelRaising_hsym` (`Ribet/LevelRaisingBG.lean:532`) proves it for a `q`-level `U` with
`Brandt.scalarE d q hq ∈ U`, where `scalarE = ι(q) · z_q⁻¹` (`Brandt/Corollaries.lean:493`): the idele that is `1` at
`q` and the scalar `q` at every other place. The proof shows `tr₁ s₀^* = T_{z_q⁻¹} T_q` and `tr₀ s₁^* = T_q`, and
`T_{z_q⁻¹} = 1` exactly when `scalarE ∈ U`.

Judgement: this is not a hidden assumption. It is the precise condition "the diamond operator `⟨q⟩` is trivial on
`X_B(U)`", it is an explicit argument, and it is proved for both levels the tex covers
(`levelRaising_hsym_Ohat` `:568`, `levelRaising_hsym_U0` `:576` for `q ∤ DM`; final statements
`alpha_alphaDagger_eq_Ohat` `:587`, `alpha_alphaDagger_eq_U0` `:599`). At `U₁(N)`-type levels the condition fails and
so does the symmetric formula, which is correct mathematics. The fibre-sum hypotheses are proved for every `q`-level
(`levelRaising_hf0/hf1`), and `levelT_eq_heckeTHom` (`:521`) identifies the abstract `T` with stage 1's `T_q`.

### 2.5 L2 (c)

`collapseDelta` (`Graph/BipCollapse.lean:388`) is defined as `∂_G` restricted to `ker collapseBoundary` and
corestricted to `K₀`. So `bip_collapse_connecting` (`:404`) is `rfl`: it restates the definition. The content of (c)
sits in other lemmas:

- `ker_collapseBoundary` / `H1_collapse2` (`:279`, `:240`): `H₁(G^{(2)}, R) = R[E]⁰`.
- `bip_collapse_connecting_unique` (`:411`): any output of the snake recipe (a lift `y'` of `y`, a `z ∈ K₀` mapping to
  `∂ y'`) equals `collapseDelta y`. Since `C₁(G) → C₁(G^{(2)})` is the identity and `K₁ = 0`, the recipe has a unique
  output, so this characterises the connecting map.
- `bip_collapse_exact` (`:419`) and `bip_collapse_surjective` (`:437`): the sequence `0 → H₁(G) → R[E]⁰ → K₀ → 0`.

Together these are the tex statement "the sequence in (a) is the long exact sequence of the collapse, with connecting
map `∂|`", except that no Lean object called "the long exact sequence" exists and the `H₀` terms are not formed. I
accept this as faithful in substance. The tex's `E ≠ ∅` is not assumed; for `E = ∅` the Lean statements are trivially
true, as the tex notes. See H1 for the presentation.
