## 1. Independence of the comparison

- **The native side is native.**
  - `jl_tree_dump.cpp` opens no files. It has no `ifstream`, `fopen`, `popen` or `system`, and it reads no reference JSON.
  - Its case lists are hard-coded inputs: (D, q, level, p, m).
  - `laneC_hj7_compare.py` reads the binary's stdout (l. 484–486) and the reference files, and nothing else. It imports no Python maths module.
  - Each `cm.eq` compares a value under `o[...]` (the binary) with one under `r[...]` (the reference). No field is compared against itself.
- **Cell coverage is complete.**
  - Validation is driven by the reference list, so a missing native cell would fail (`present`).
  - The other suites iterate over the native cells. I counted the reference cells independently: T2 = 3, T3 = 3, T1 = 5, T6 at k = 2 = 4, H_Vk = 15 cases + 15 acceptance rows at k = 2, validation = 240. These match the 288 compared cells.
- **Matrices are compared after an independent vertex permutation.** `find_perm` ends with an exact check of the labelled edge multiset (l. 190), so it cannot report a false isomorphism. H, H′, Hᵀ, αα† (weighted, unweighted, Ribet sign) and the Eisenstein vector are then compared entrywise under π.
- **Some fields are weak or tautological, but a minority.** See minor findings m1–m3; they are a few percent of the 3744 fields. Every cell still contains substantive native data: X, E, masses, stabiliser multisets, graph isomorphism, Smith data, and the matrices.

## 2. The maths

- **s₁ and the transport are correct.**
  - Write J = I_{c′}μ/N_{c′}. Then nrd μ = q·N_c·N_{c′}, and the isomorphism J → I_{c′} is x ↦ x·μ⁻¹·N_{c′} = x·conj(μ)/(q N_c).
  - On level data v ∈ I_c we have qv ∈ J (since qI_c ⊂ J), and qv ↦ v·conj(μ)/N_c. This is the code's map (`Tree.cpp:614-620`, `rmul` divides by N_c at l. 464-468).
  - It equals v·g⁻¹ with g = μ/(qN_{c′}). The factor q is the diamond twist; it matters at U₁ and not at U₀.
  - The type-preserving permutation used in the T3 U₁(5), U₁(25) cells would detect a wrong diamond, and they match.
- **αα† is correct** (`Tree.cpp:784-806`).
  - With ⟨x,x⟩ = w_x and ⟨e,e⟩ = w_e, the adjoint is α†[x]_j = ±Σ_{e: s_j e = x} (w_x/w_e)·e.
  - The diagonal blocks are Σ_{e∋x} w_x/w_e = q+1 (the fibre sum).
  - The upper-right block is −Σ_{s₀e=a, s₁e=b} w_b/w_e = −H′, and the lower-left block is −Hᵀ.
  - The B₁₁ matrix [[3,0,−1,−3],[0,3,−2,0],[−1,−3,3,0],[−2,0,0,3]] agrees with this, and the `sign_check` booleans are computed, not asserted.
- **The cuspidal part is sound.**
  - Cusp = {Σf₀ = Σf₁ = 0} (unweighted). It is M-stable because every column of M has constant block sums: (q+1, −(q+1)) for type-0 columns and (−(q+1), q+1) for type-1 columns. This uses the fibre sums at s₀ and at s₁.
  - Over Q, Cusp is exactly the λ ≠ q+1 part. M acts on divisors through Hᵀ, whose column sums are q+1, so the unweighted degree is the right one.
  - For B₁₁ the λ = −2 block has det (q+1)² − λ² = 5, so |coker M|_Cusp| = 5 independently of the basis. "Cyclic" is an integral statement that holds for this lattice.
  - Caveat: the choice of lattice is a convention. The Eisenstein-quotient reading gives 30. The WP note says this openly and labels the result NEW.
- **Φ^comb matches the plan** (`Tree.cpp:810-840`).
  - It is the Smith form of the w_e-weighted Gram on a ℤ-basis of ker ∂ (`kernel_basis`).
  - The collapsed two-vertex graph uses ker = {Σa = 0}. For B₁₁, weights (2,1,1) give Gram [[3,2],[2,3]], so Z/5, as claimed.
  - The Mazur T1 orders match num((q−1)/12).
- **The Ihara map is correct.** ι = (s₀^*, −s₁^*) (`Tree.cpp:776-783`). Its kernel is the diagonal and its coker is torsion-free at B₁₁ (NEW, not compared).
