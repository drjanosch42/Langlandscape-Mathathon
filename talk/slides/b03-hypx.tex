\begin{frame}{Hyp.X: the one geometric hypothesis}
  \small
  \textbf{What it states} \stattag{hypothesis}. For $D=q$ and the levels $\Gamma_1(Mp^s)\cap\Gamma_0(q)$, $s\ge0$: the character group
  $X_q(J_s)$ of the toric part of the special fibre at $q$ is the degree-zero Brandt module of $B_q$, i.e.\ the DR graph of $B_q$
  (edges $=$ the class set, weights $w_e$) \emph{is} the dual graph of the special fibre at $q$, compatibly with
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
    \item Hecke operators (X1), the monodromy pairing (X2) and the tower maps (X3): \textbf{Hyp.X.char};
    \item the Galois action, i.e.\ the monodromy filtration on the Tate module (X4): \textbf{Hyp.X.gal}.
  \end{itemize}
  \textbf{Status.} At $\Gamma_0$-type level this is Ribet's theorem \stattag{cited: Deligne--Rapoport, Deuring, Grothendieck};
  at $\Gamma_1(Mp^s)$ it is expected by the same geometry, without JL \stattag{expected; not carried out};
  beyond $D=q$ the literature uses JL.

  \textbf{Role.} The main defect theorem does \emph{not} use it. Given Hyp.X.char, the $\Lambda$-adic character group is
  $\cong M^{\mathrm{ord}}$ \stattag{proved given Hyp.X.char}. Its simplicial form, an explicit last arrow, is conjecture O2 \stattag{conjectural; open}.
\end{frame}
