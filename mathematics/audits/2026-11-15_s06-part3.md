## 1. Item (1): case (γ) is empty (`s06-m5.tex:233–235`, `\stproved`)

**Claim.** Let ℓ | N(ρ̄), ℓ ≠ p, and d(ℓ) := dim ρ̄^{I_ℓ} = 1. Then ρ̄(I_ℓ) is a non-trivial unipotent group, so p | #ρ̄(I_ℓ).
Hence case (γ) (d(ℓ) = 1 and p ∤ #ρ̄(I_ℓ)) does not occur, and (β) is the whole case d(ℓ) = 1.

**Proof (own derivation; status: proved).**
1. *The determinant.* f has trivial character, so det ρ_f = χ_cyc^{1−k} in the chapter's convention (geometric Frobenius,
   `s06-m5.tex:18`), and det ρ̄ = ω^{1−k}. The sign of the exponent plays no role: every power of ω is unramified at ℓ ≠ p. Here
   ℓ ≠ p because ℓ | M and p ∤ N. So det ρ̄ is trivial on I_ℓ.
2. *The shape.* Let L = ρ̄^{I_ℓ}, a line. In a basis (e₁, e₂) with e₁ ∈ L, ρ̄(σ) = [[1, b(σ)], [0, χ(σ)]] for σ ∈ I_ℓ, and
   χ(σ) = det ρ̄(σ) = 1. So ρ̄(I_ℓ) lies in the group of upper unipotent matrices, which is isomorphic to (𝔽, +), an elementary
   abelian p-group. This uses no semisimplicity and no assumption on the order of the image.
3. *Non-trivial.* If b = 0, then ρ̄(I_ℓ) = 1 and d(ℓ) = 2. So b ≠ 0, ρ̄(I_ℓ) is a non-trivial p-group, and p | #ρ̄(I_ℓ).

**The points raised by the stage master.**
- *Is (γ) empty, or does it reduce to a case covered elsewhere?* It is empty as defined: its two conditions d(ℓ) = 1 and
  p ∤ #ρ̄(I_ℓ) contradict each other. Nothing has to be moved to another case. The representations with d(ℓ) = 1 are exactly the
  "Steinberg-type" ones, ρ̄|G_ℓ ≅ μ̄ ⊗ [[ω, ∗], [0, 1]] up to the order of the characters, with μ̄ unramified and ∗ ramified, and they
  are all in (β) by definition (d(ℓ) = 1, p | #ρ̄(I_ℓ)). So (α) (c(ℓ) = 0), (δ) (d(ℓ) = 0), (β) (d(ℓ) = 1) cover every ℓ | M.
- *ℓ ≡ −1 mod p.* The argument is about I_ℓ only and uses no congruence on ℓ. The primes of P_ρ̄ (ℓ ≡ −1, ρ̄|G_ℓ irreducible) have
  d(ℓ) = 0 and are in (δ). For ℓ ≡ −1 with d(ℓ) = 1 the residual sign ambiguity exists only for ℓ ∈ Σ⁻ (`rem:s06-levM-sign`);
  in (β) the sign residue is the Frobenius eigenvalue on the line ρ̄^{I_ℓ}, which ρ̄ determines.
- *Wild inertia, p | #ρ̄(I_ℓ).* Wild inertia is pro-ℓ and ρ̄(I_ℓ) is a p-group, ℓ ≠ p, so wild inertia acts trivially: ρ̄|I_ℓ is tame,
  Sw = 0, c(ℓ) = (2 − 1) + 0 = 1. This is the first sentence of the proof of (β) (`s06-m5.tex:258`), and it is correct. A wildly
  ramified ρ̄ at ℓ therefore has d(ℓ) = 0 (case (δ)). ℓ = 2 is included (p is odd).
- *Consistency with the old text.* The proof of (γ) itself (`:261–269`) already contained the remark "unipotent of order prime to
  p, hence trivial"; it is now vacuous and harmless. Audit part 2, §2–§3, re-derived (γ) without noticing that it is empty; the
  statements made there stay true.

**Consequence for the text.** The sentence in `rem:s06-levM-open` is to be replaced (P3-R1). Three other places still speak of
(γ) as a live case (P3-R2, P3-R3).
