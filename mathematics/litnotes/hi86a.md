## What is where (for a future citation of the Lambda-adic Galois representation, `def:s06-tam`)
1. **Thm 1.1, p. 551:** for every k >= 2 the restriction of operators induces an isomorphism of the ordinary Lambda-adic Hecke algebra (modulo the usual weight-k kernel) with the weight-k ordinary Hecke algebra for Gamma_1(N p^r), taking T(l) to T(l) etc. (the "control theorem"; the Hecke algebras are defined before it, pp. 549-551).
2. **Thm 1.2, p. 553; Cor. 1.3-1.6, pp. 554-555:** structure of the primitive components and weight-k specialisations; Cor. 1.5 attaches to each ordinary form of weight k >= 2 a non-trivial algebra homomorphism from the Hecke algebra to the coefficient ring (this is the "Cor. 1.5" quoted at the start of Sect. 2, p. 557). The contents of Thm 1.2, Cor. 1.3, 1.4, 1.6 were identified only from their first lines.
3. **Thm 2.1, p. 557 (PDF 13):** for a primitive local ring of the Hecke algebra there is a continuous, simple, representation of G_Q into GL_2 of the corresponding coefficient ring (a normal domain finite over Lambda) that is unramified outside Np and whose reduction modulo each height-one prime P_f (f an ordinary form of weight k >= 2 in the component) is equivalent to the Galois representation rho(f) attached to f. This is the Lambda-adic Galois representation rho_F of tex `def:s06-tam` (Hida, "Hida family Galois representation"). The text layer garbles the target ring (the symbols for "the integral closure ring" are lost); read it from the image before citing the exact ring.
4. **Lemma 8.1, p. 596 (PDF 52):** the module V_K (of ordinary Lambda-adic forms tensor K) is free of rank 2 over the Hecke algebra T(N;K) (proof via Thm 3.1 and Lemma 6.4); this is the Hida input behind [MW86, Sect. 8, 9], and the freeness of rank two of H over T that stage 06 gets from [Hs] / [Wil95]. Thm 3.1 (p. 562, PDF 18) is the structure of the Lambda-module of ordinary forms.
5. Further: Thm 4.4 (p. 568) identity for Hecke operators; Thm 5.8 (p. 584) duality; Cor. 6.5 (p. 588): Frobenius algebra property of d_k(Gamma_1(M);K) for k >= 2 (the Gorenstein-type input before Wiles); Thm 9.3, 9.4 (pp. 600, 603), Thm 10.5 (p. 609).
